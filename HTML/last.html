6. CONCLUSION

If we look at how the space industry found things like the "Neptunian Desert" or cotton-candy super-puffs, they didn't spot them just by looking through a telescope lens. They found them using data visualization and statistical correlation. When astronomers mapped orbital periods against planet sizes, the scatter plots literally showed a blank space where intermediate planets get vaporized by stellar radiation, along with weird outlier planets that floated way above normal density trends.

At the same time, working on this made it pretty clear where traditional plotting hits a wall. Sure, a scatter plot is awesome for a quick visual, but it can only show a couple of variables at a time. If we are dealing with thousands of messy, multi-parameter telescope logs, human-made graphs and basic correlation checks will eventually miss subtle patterns hidden deep in the noise.

That is exactly why the space sector relies heavily on deep learning and neural networks today. Modern AI models can sift through millions of data points and spot faint, multi-dimensional signals that standard correlation graphs would completely overlook. Writing scripts and generating scatter plots is a great foundation, but AI is ultimately what’s driving modern space discovery forward.
